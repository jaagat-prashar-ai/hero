# The three claim types, using that example sentence: 

# PerceptualClaim: "i see x." an assertion that some entity exists in the scene, optinally with a state: there is a pedestrian, and it is crossing. This is checkable against the obstacle.offlien actor tracks in the datset - was there really a pedestrian there?
# CommitmentClaim: "i will do y." A maneuever or speed profile the model says it's going to execute: slow down, keep straight. This is checkable against the trajectory the model actually produced (its TrajectoryFeature) - did it really slow down?
# CausalClaim: - the "because" glue linking th etwo: I'm slowing down because of th epedestrian. It ties one or more commitments (the effects) to the preceptions that supposedlt causex them. This is what lets you catch reasoning that's unfaithful even when its parts are individually true (e.g., the pedestrian exists and the4 card di dslow, but the stated cause-effect link doesn't hold up)

# Perceptiual is what the model claims tos ee, comitment is what it does, causal is why it claims one led to the other (parser exists so each of those can be fact-checked by code instead of by a human or another LLM).

# how does the parser work?

# rule-based pipeline (regexes and lexcions tuned against ~2000 real CoC strings from pref_pairs/results_scene_reasoning/*md).

# Example sentence: "Nudge left due to construction cones blocking th eright side of our lane, then accelerate"

# Step 1: Split this into beats. The text is split on :, then, or sentence-ending periods (_split_beats). Each beat is one plan step. 
Here the beat 1 is "Nudge left due to construction cones blocking...," beat 2 is "accelerate." 

# Step 2: split each beat on its causal connective. Within a beat, _split_beat looks for "because / due to / since / as" (with "after / for" as a waker fallback tier, only used if no string connective exist). Everything before the connective is the commitment clause (what ego will do), everything after is the cause clause (why). 
A beat with no connective is fine (it's just a commitment with no reason).
# Thought: does this also work for the prefix idea? look more into this!!!!!!!!!!

# Step 3: extract commitments from the commitemnt clause only. MANEUVER_PATTERSN (~17 regexes: nudge, lane change, merge, accelerate, stop, yield, ...) are scanned over the commitment side. Each match becomes a CommitmentClaim with a canonical manauever key, an axis (lateral vs longitudinal), and optionally a direction: "left/right" is searched in a 40-char windo after the verb, capped at the next maneuever verb so compound commitments like "change lanes to the right and enter the ramp" don't both grab the ame "right." Scopin to the commitment side is delibearte: it prevents "vehicle merging from the right" in a cause clause from being read as ego's own merge. 

# Step 4: Extract perceptual claims from the whole beat. ENTITY_PATTERNS (~25 regexes: cones, pedestrian, stopped_vehicle, signal, work_zone, ...) find entity mentions, then each entity is paried with the nearest matching state_word from STATE_PATTERNS (blocking, crossing, red, green, ...). It scans the whole beat reather than just the cause clause because entity and state sometimes straddle the connective ("Adapt speed for the roundabout since a yield-controlled enty is ahead"). 

# Step 5: Assemble causal claims. If a connective was found, the beat becomes one CausalClaim: effects = the beat's commitments, cause = the subest of its perceptual claims whose span sits after teh connective. So here nudge(left) <- "due to" <- construction_cones (blocking).

    # Dossier.py:  
        # Inputs, all from the clip's ground truth:
        - the clip's obstacle tracks (every object's position over time),
        - the expert driver's trajectory (what the human actually drove),
        - the GT reasoning sentence ("Decelerate to maintain a safe distance...").

    # Processing:
    1. Summarize each obstacle track to one line — for each track, compute when it's visible, its closest approach to the ego (distance + time), and its direction at that moment (ahead/left/right/behind, simple angle buckets). Keep the 12 nearest tracks, sorted closest-first.
    2. Summarize what the expert did in ~4 lines — initial/min/final speed and when the minimum happened, total speed drop, distance covered, final lateral offset, and whether a stop/yield occurred.
    3. Append the GT reasoning sentence verbatim.


dossier.py contains six pieces:

1. _bearing(x, y) — angle of a point in the ego frame → bucket: within ±20° = "ahead", beyond ±120° = "behind", else "left"/"right".
2. summarize_tracks(scene) — for each annotated obstacle track: compute distance √(x²+y²) at every sample, take min → closest approach (m) and its time; first/last timestamp → visibility window; _bearing at the closest moment. Sort all tracks by closest approach, keep nearest 12.
3. waypoints_from_egomotion(df, hz=10) — raw egomotion log → (N, 2) waypoint array: finds the timestamp/x/y columns across naming variants, converts µs→s, interpolates x and y onto a uniform 10 Hz grid.
4. ego_lines(traj) — 4–5 summary lines from the expert's TrajectoryFeatures: duration + distance; initial/min/final speed with the time of the min and total drop; final and max lateral offset; stop/yield event flags; heading change only if distance ≥ 5 m, otherwise a "nearly stationary" note.
5. build_dossier(scene, gt_traj, gt_coc) — renders the final text: clip ID, one line per track from (2) (- track 32 [trailer] visible 0.0–19.8 s; closest 8.2 m (ahead) at t=17.5 s), a note if tracks were omitted, the ego lines from (4), then the GT reasoning sentence verbatim. Returns one ~25-line string — this is what the generator LLM reads as Step 1.
6. features_from_waypoints(...) — thin wrapper around extract_features for callers outside pref_pairs.

# "No-react" is the no_reaction_traj corruption (the do nothing counterfactual).
# Construction (flattened_waypoints in gate.py): take the GT trajectory's first step only - its initial velocity - and extraploate it for the whole clip. 
# The result is a car that keeps driving at its starting speed and heading, in a straight line, forever. 
# It never breaks, never swerves, never stops. That trajectory gets paired with the unchagned GT claims. 

# Perturbation recap (this can be helpful for perturbation generator/discriminator thing): 
    # gate.build_perturbations for each clip - one positive plus four corruptions, all derived from the same ground-truth pair (GT claims + GT trajectory), each deleting or distorting exactly one thing: 
        Case: positive
        Claims: GT
        Trajectory: GT
        How it's built: untouched pair
        The exploit it catches: (baseline - must score >= 0.7)


        Case: reversed
        Claims: GT 
        Trajectory: GT played backwards
        How it's built: waypoints[::-1], featuers re-extracted
        The exploit it catches: Checking aggregate stats (min/max speed, total drop) instead of temporal order - a stop-then-park looks like launch-from-park

        Case: no-react
        Claims: GT 
        Trajectory: constant-velocity extraploation
        How it's built: first-step velocity continued straight for the whole clip 
        The exploit it catches: Requiring no reaction at all

        - reward for narrating the maneuever while never braking

        Case: gutted
        Claims: all claims deleted
        Trajectory: GT 
        How it's built: perceptual = [], commitments = [], causal = [] 
        The exploit it catches: Pure trajectory-matching - reward for driving correctly with no reasoning

        Case: no-commit
        Claims: comitments + causal deleted, perception kept 
        Trajectory: GT 
        How it's built: commitments=[], causal=[]
        The exploit it catches: Mention-only credit: "I see the pedestrian" with no commitment to act still scores 
        # Pass criterion: positive >= 0.7, and every corruption >= 0.4 below the positive. 

        # Three design properties worth remembering: 
            # 1. Two-sided by construction. Rows 2-3 corrupt the action while keeping the words (right claims, wrong trajectory); rows 4-5 corrupt the words while keeping the action (right trajectory, missing claims). 
            # Together they enforce the full faithfulness chain: register -> commit -> execute - because each corruption servers one link. 

            # 2. Trajectory corruptions transform waypoints, not features. The reversed/flatteneed waypoints are re-run through the same feature extractor, so speeds, headings, and derived fields stay internally consistent instead of being hand-edited into something no real trajectory could produce. 
            # 3. Degenerate cases are screened out. If a transformed trajectory deviates < 2 m from the original (__too_similar), it's skipped - on the near-stationary pedestrian clip both reversed and no-react reproduce the parked ground truth, so that clip is gated on the two claims corruptions only. 
            # 4. Similarly, no-commit is only built if the claims actually contain commitments to delete. 

        # And the forward-looking property: nothing in this construction is specific to ground truth. Point the same function at a policy's rollout claims and waypoints, and the battery becomes your selection-time verifier - corrupt the argmax rollout, demand the drops, trust it only if the reward functions can tell it from its own corruptions.
