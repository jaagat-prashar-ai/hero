
# CLIP-style scoring: two small learned encoders map each modality into a shared embedding SPACE
# "Score" is just the dot product there

# Text side: for each of the K instructions, pool the LLM's hidden states over the instruction tokens (mean-pool), pass through a small projection MLP -> vector z_text (e.g., 256-d, L2-normalized)
# Trajectory side: take the K predicted waypoint sequences [F, 2], flatten, pass through a small MLP -> z_traj (same dim, normalzied). 
# Score matrix: within one frame's group, S[i][j] = z_text[i] times z_traj[j] / t - a KxK matrix of scores
# Loss: cross entropy pushing the diagonal up, row-wise (instruction i -> which trajectory?) and coliumn wise (trajectoru j -> which instruction?). The column-wise direction is the inverse head, symmetric InfoNCE gives you trajectory-instruction retrieval for free, no separate decoder. 

All K samples share thes ame image, the image features are identical across the group, the only way to make S diagonal is for the instruction to causally shape the predicted trajectory and for the text representation to encode its action consequence. Gradient flows through both encoders through the watpoiny predictions themselves.

group_ids is just that last column — [0,0,0,0,1]. It's how the loss knows positions 0–3 are four variants of the same camera image ("counterfactual siblings") while position 4 is alone ("singleton"). Without it, the model couldn't tell which samples to compare against each other.

For each sample, we make two vectors: z_text (summary of the instruction) and z_traj (summary of the trajectory the model predicted). 
#Within group 0, we score every instruction against every trajectory with a dot product. Large when two vectors point the same way and we divide by a temperature 0.07 to sharpen softmax.

Loss function is cross-entropy, treated as a classification problem. Take the first row: "here are 4 trajecotires, which one belongs to speed up?"
# The correct answer is column 1. Cross-entropy rewards put the highest score on the daigonal. That's InfoNCE - softmax-classification where "classes" are siblign trajectories.

    # The loss that actually updates the network's weights is total loss: L_total = L_language + L_waypoints + L_route + 0.5 x L_contrastive

    L_langauge: cross-entropy, next-token prediction on the answer text (standard LLM loss)
    L_waypoints: smooth-L1 (Huber) between the 10 predicted future waypoints and the target waypoints. 
    # L_route: smooth-L1 on the 20 predicted route points
    # L_contrastive: our symmetric InfoNCE (the cosine-similarity matching game from before)

    # Then a single L_total.backward() computes gradients, and AdamW updates every trainable parameter with them: the LoRA adapters on the LLM, the vision encoder, the waypoints/route prediction heads, and our two new projection MLPs (text_proj, traj_proj). 
    # There's no separate optimizer for the contrastive part. All four terms pull on the same shared parameters at once: the waypoint loss says "predict the correct trajectory," the contrastive loss simultaneously says "and make it distngishably reflect the instruction."
